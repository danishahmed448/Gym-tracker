Progression & safety

  • RIR standard: stop 1–2 reps shy of failure on most sets. On an 1800 kcal cut, grinding every set to failure adds fatigue you can't fully recover from.
  • Controlled eccentrics: take a strict 3-second lowering on your first exercise or two each day — the heavier compound lifts. On the higher-rep isolation work, stay controlled but don't force the full 3 seconds; those sets are long enough already.
  • Calf rule: 2-second dead pause at full bottom stretch on leg press calf raises.
  • Progression: once every set of a lift hits the top of its rep range with good form, two sessions running, add the smallest jump available — log today's weight in the field next to each exercise so you can see it next time.
  • Cycle sequence: Chest & Back → Legs & Forearm → Shoulders & Arms → Rest → repeat. Tap "Finished today's session" at the bottom of a day to log it and clear the checkboxes for next time.

Matching your diet

1800 kcal with 130g protein already supports holding onto muscle in a deficit. 140–150g (~2g/kg) is a legitimate upper range, but ease into it gradually rather than jumping straight there — your last bloodwork put uric acid at the top of the reference range, and your protein is entirely chicken. Increase slowly, keep hydration up, and it's worth rechecking that number if you hold protein higher for a while.

Maintenance is roughly 2200–2400 kcal for your height and weight, so 1800 kcal is a moderate 400–600 kcal deficit — enough to reach 68kg in about 7–10 weeks without eating into training.

If your lifts stall across the board or you feel flat for more than a week, bring calories back near maintenance for a couple weeks before continuing the cut.
